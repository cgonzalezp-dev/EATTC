Chapter 4 · The hard questions

"Isn't he overqualified for this seat?"

If someone close to Josh raises a concern about a newcomer with a senior profile in an assistant role, they would be right to. These are the six questions I would ask in their place, and what I would commit to for each.

1Will he leave when something bigger appears?
"A director-level profile won't stay in this seat."
My answer: I am choosing this role for what it gives me: a seat next to a CEO, learning how a tax and accounting firm runs. I would agree on a 6-month and 12-month check-in with Josh, and a notice commitment in writing.
Josh can verify: 8 years at one company, promoted at every stage.
2Will he try to run things instead of supporting?
"He'll overstep and make decisions that aren't his."
My answer: I recommend, Josh decides. In week one I would ask for an explicit map of what I do alone, what I do and report, and what I ask first (see the ladder below).
Josh can verify: every decision I bring has options and a recommendation, never a surprise.
3Are the numbers on his résumé inflated?
"Big claims, but who confirms them?"
My answer: Ask me anything. I would offer references, and a paid work-sample week on real tasks (calendar, a meeting recap, an SOP) so Josh sees quality before committing.
Josh can verify: references and a trial task, on his terms.
4Can he do the actual job from day one?
"No assistant title. Doesn't know our tools."
My answer: True, I have not held the title and I have not used ClickUp, Slack, Loom or Missive at work. I would complete a tool-readiness sprint before day one and ship my first SOP in week one. My experience is in the work behind it: executive reviews, action items, follow-through.
Josh can verify: a live demo of my calendar and recap workflow.
5Can he be trusted with sensitive client data?
"A new person with access to tax and financial files."
My answer: Least-privilege access that grows with trust, a signed confidentiality agreement, and no data outside company tools. I have handled regulated data privacy requirements (HIPAA) in my current work.
Josh can verify: access is granted and reviewed by him, step by step.
6Will he create friction with the team around Josh?
"A newcomer with a strong profile can become a rival."
My answer: I would meet each person in Josh's circle in the first two weeks, ask how they want to work with me, and share credit. No side channels: nothing reaches Josh about the team that the team would not hear from me first.
Josh can verify: ask his circle at day 30 and day 90.

Trust is earned in steps, so autonomy grows in steps

Josh stays in control at every stage. This is the ladder I would propose.

LEVEL 1 · DO

Without asking

  • Calendar holds and reminders
  • Meeting prep and recaps
  • Tracking tasks and owners
LEVEL 2 · DO AND TELL

Act, then report

  • Reschedule low-risk meetings
  • Draft replies for review
  • Chase team updates
LEVEL 3 · ASK FIRST

Josh decides

  • Anything external in his name
  • Priorities and commitments
  • Client or financial data decisions
